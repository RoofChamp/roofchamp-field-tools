What to Text Them, and When

Roof Champ Columbus · 21 messages, in claim order

How to use theseSend them one at a time, at the moment listed. Adjust so it sounds like you: the shape is what matters, not the exact words.
  • Long-press a grey message to select the whole thing, then Copy. That works everywhere, even with no internet.
  • These are bullet points, not emails. The homeowner writes it in their own words. Never hand them something to paste.
  • One sentence is the exception. "If there's anything else you require from me, will you please let me know?". Word for word, as its own message so they can screenshot it.
  • Nothing here interprets a policy. Coverage questions go to their carrier.

The early questions
6After the carrier's first replyWithin a day of their reply, still before the inspection.›
Script 02
They wrote back. Good: now's the moment for a couple of easy questions, while everybody's still agreeable. Reply to that same email and cover: • Thank them, say you look forward to the inspection • "I know I have a deductible. Should I expect to spend any other money out of pocket?" • "Is it fair to say we both want the work done to current code and in a workmanlike manner?" Then the usual closing sentence. These cost them nothing to answer right now. That's exactly why we ask now.

12"We only owe for direct physical damage"Any coverage-flavored deflection.›
Script 05
Don't argue the policy with them. That's a fight nobody wins by email. Ask about the roof instead, since that's the part everybody can see. Cover these: • You're not a policy expert, you'd rather understand the outcome than the wording • "I thought the purpose of the claim was to put my property back the way it was before the storm." • "If only the damaged shingles were replaced, would my roof look the way it did beforehand, or worse?" • "If it would look worse, I don't believe I've been made whole. Can you help me understand how we get there?" They have to say worse.
